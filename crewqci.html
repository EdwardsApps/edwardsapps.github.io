The problem

The money leaks between the quote and the bank.

A quote goes out and nobody chases it. A retention falls due next March and nobody diarises it. A client's balance creeps past the limit you set two years ago and nobody notices until the invoice is already out. None of that happens because anyone is careless — it happens because the quote, the invoice, the retention and the credit limit live in four different places, and nothing joins them up.

CrewQCI puts them in one register, so a won quote becomes a contract, the contract becomes documents, and the money owed against it is visible the whole way through.

What it does

Lead to quote to contract to invoice to retention.

  • Every enquiry in one pipeline

    Leads carry their source, sector and owner, so the follow-up is somebody's job rather than everybody's. Convert a lead to a quote and nothing is re-typed.

  • Quotes that chase themselves into view

    Quote numbers allocate in sequence. Statuses run from Quoted through Emailed and Called Follow Up to Won or Lost, colour-coded down the register so anything gone cold is visible from across the office — and a won quote becomes a contract in one click.

  • Invoices, credit notes and CIS reverse charge

    Raise invoices and credit notes against the contract as branded PDFs, emailed under your own company name. Mark a client CIS registered and the domestic reverse charge is applied and worded properly on everything that follows.

  • Proformas, applications and payment certificates

    Applications for payment are numbered per contract with previous and current valuations, and certify into a payment certificate and VAT invoice when the money is agreed — the way main-contractor work actually gets paid.

  • Retentions you actually get back

    Raise an invoice with a retention percentage and the retention posts itself to its own register with the amount held and the date it falls due, then counts down in front of you — amber as it approaches, red once it is overdue.

  • Credit limits and live exposure

    Every client's outstanding balance is computed from the invoice register, net of VAT, and set against the limit you have given them. Staff can request more headroom and an admin approves or declines it, with the decision emailed back.

  • Insights and reports, straight off the register

    Conversion by sector and source, quoted against won by month, and aged debt taken from the same invoices the documents were printed from — so the report and the register can never disagree. Export to PDF, Excel or CSV, including a Sage-ready sales ledger with nominal codes per sector.

  • A client register that keeps itself tidy

    Addresses, contacts, Sage codes, sector and credit limit in one place. Change a detail once and it propagates across the contract and its documents, and any name used on a record with no matching account is flagged for setting up.

Where it comes from

It started life as a spreadsheet that worked.

CrewQCI was not designed from interviews with contractors. It grew out of the quotes, costs and invoices workbook of a working UK surfacing and groundworks firm — the numbering, the statuses, the row colours and the VAT handling all came straight out of it. That is why it knows what an application for payment is, why CIS reverse charge is a checkbox rather than a support ticket, and why retentions get their own register instead of a note in the margin.

The difference from the spreadsheet is that it chases, prints, emails and adds up on its own — and two people can be in it at once without one of them overwriting the other.
