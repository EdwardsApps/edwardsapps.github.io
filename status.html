    Good to know

    How this page works

    Is it my internet or the service?

    Look for the service above. If it shows a problem, the fault is theirs and nothing at home or in the office will fix it. If it shows no problems, try a different website and then try your phone on mobile data instead of wifi. If other sites work and mobile data works, the trouble is probably your own connection or device.

    Where does the information come from?

    Mostly from each company’s own public status feed, which is the same information behind their official status page. For a few services that publish nothing, such as X, Netflix and TikTok, we run our own simple test to see whether they answer. Each card says which it is and links to the official page where there is one.

    How often is it updated?

    About every 15 minutes, automatically. The time of the last check is shown at the top of the page, and the page refreshes itself while you have it open.

    It says no problems, but it isn’t working for me

    Companies are often slow to admit a fault, so their status can lag behind what people are seeing. A green light here means the company has not reported a problem, not that one is impossible. It can also be a local fault that only affects you or your area.

    Why is there no official status for WhatsApp, Facebook or Instagram?

    Meta does not publish one for the everyday apps. We do the next best thing: we test whether each one answers, and we read Meta’s official status for the business tools built on top of them, which usually shows trouble during a big outage.

    What do the labels mean?

    No problems reported means the company’s feed is clear. Some problems means part of the service is affected or it is slow. Major problems means a serious or widespread fault. Status unavailable means we could not read their feed just now.

    Why isn’t my bank or broadband here?

    Most high-street banks, mobile networks and broadband firms do not publish a status feed that can be read automatically. We only show services where the information is dependable. If there is one you would like added, tell us.

    Who runs this?

    EdwardsApps, a small UK software studio. The page is independent and is not connected with any of the companies listed. Their names belong to them. The checks are automatic and nobody edits the results by hand. It is a guide, so check the company’s own page before making a decision that matters.
