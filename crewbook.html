What it does

One place for the working week.

  • Scheduling and job tracking

    Every job, every crew, every day in one view. Move a job and the rest of the picture moves with it, so nobody is working from last week's whiteboard.

  • The whole job on one card

    Open any job and it is all there — client and contact, coordinator, crew, owned plant, booked materials with delivery times, hired plant with day rates, and the site notes the crew need. Nothing to chase round the office.

  • Labour, materials and plant

    Day rates, materials and plant go against the job as it happens, rather than from memory a fortnight later — so a job's real cost is visible while there is still time to act on it.

  • Invoicing, including proforma

    Invoices and proformas are built straight from the job, using the numbers captured on site rather than re-typed later — so the invoice matches the work.

  • CIS-aware Sage 50 export

    CrewBook routes every invoice line the way CIS expects — labour netted for deduction and materials paid gross, gross-status lump sums left untouched, and non-CIS work left alone — then exports it clean to Sage 50, which takes the re-keying out of month end.

  • WhatsApp job notifications

    Crews get the job where they already look: WhatsApp. Where, when and what is needed, sent from the schedule rather than typed out each evening.

  • The emails that send themselves

    Each coordinator gets tomorrow's operations brief by email every day — crew, materials, plant and credit status for their jobs. The weekly wage summary lands with the office on Mondays, and holiday approvals notify the right people. Set them up once and they carry on.

  • Wage sheets, straight from the schedule

    The week's shifts become the week's wage sheet — split days, sick, yard and holiday all accounted for, basic and charge rates side by side, exported to Excel in one click.

  • Expense claims with the receipt attached

    Crews claim diesel, permits and PPE with the receipt attached and the job number against the line; the office approves, rejects or pays with the VAT already split out.

  • Mileage, logged and claimed

    Journeys are logged against the job, then turned into expense claims in one click — batch the week's trips or claim them one at a time.

  • Holidays in one tracker

    Requests, approvals and allowances together — approved leave shows on the calendar, pending requests are flagged, and remaining days count themselves.

Built in: QCI

The sales side, built in.

QCI is CrewBook's sales side — close to a second application inside the app. Leads, quotes and chase-ups, contracts, invoicing, proformas and applications for payment, retentions and credit control each get their own register, and the pipeline runs the way contracting actually works: lead → quote → contract → invoice → chase → retention.

Because it lives inside CrewBook, nothing is re-typed. A won quote becomes a contract the schedule already knows about, invoices land against the same job the costs were captured on, and Costings shows sales against cost per contract without anyone reconciling spreadsheets.

And it chases the money. Every open invoice ages from its due date and climbs a ladder you set — a reminder before it falls due, then chasers at 7, 21 and 45 days over, one email per client per run, under your company name. It knows when not to, as well: a client who has promised to pay by Friday is left alone until Friday, and so is one on hold, one under your threshold or one already chased this week. Statements of account go out monthly as a PDF, matured retention gets its own release request, and a client who clears their account gets a thank-you. Test mode redirects everything to your own address until you clear it, a dry run tells you what today would send without sending it, and every email is logged so a stage is not fired twice.
